Fix & Flip · transaction flow

Purchase → Rehab → ARV → Exit

A fix-and-flip file is a short business plan: buy right, renovate with a controlled budget, defend after-repair value, and exit by sale or refinance. RECR helps match that plan to private-lender and hard-money options — not as the automatic direct funder.

  1. 1 · PurchaseContract price, condition, and basis you can actually close.
  2. 2 · RehabScoped budget, timeline, and who executes the work.
  3. 3 · ARVDefensible comps for the finished product — not hope pricing.
  4. 4 · ExitResale or refinance that repays the stack on a realistic date.
  • Capital use Acquisition plus renovation draws for a planned resale or refinance
  • Contribution Borrower cash-in and reserves are program-specific — never assumed
  • Leverage Reviewed against cost, ARV support, and exit — confirmed per lender/program
  • Draws Rehab typically funds against verified completed work, not a lump sum
  • Collateral Investment SFR, many condos, and small residential multi within program rules
  • RECR role Path fit, document readiness, and private-lender matching for the file

Often a fit when

  • Scope, budget, and ARV can be stated without hand-waving
  • Exit is a sale or refinance with a credible timeline
  • Business-purpose, non-owner-occupied use

Often not a fit when

  • ARV is retail list price with no supporting comps
  • Rehab budget is a round number without line items
  • Exit depends on an undefined “buyer later”
Rental / DSCR · formula explainer

Income ÷ Payment → Hold Strategy

DSCR financing looks at whether the property’s income can support the debt service — not whether your W-2 clears a consumer mortgage formula. The conversation is about rents, expenses, reserves, and a hold or refinance plan.

Property income ÷ PITIA / debt service → DSCR

DSCR is a coverage ratio: how many times the property’s net operating income covers the full housing payment used in underwriting (often including principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and HOA where applicable). Exact minimum ratios are set by the lender/program under review — RECR does not publish a fixed floor here.

  • Who it fits Investors buying or refinancing 1–4 unit investment rentals to hold for cash flow
  • Income story In-place or market rents that can be documented and defended
  • Payment story Realistic taxes, insurance, and reserves — not a bare principal-and-interest fantasy
  • Strategy Long-term hold, BRRRR takeout, or rate/term refinance of a performing rental
  • Contribution Down payment, cash-out limits, and reserves confirmed per program
  • RECR role Stress-test the ratio, package the file, match to DSCR-capable private-lender options

Have ready before path review

Address · purchase or refinance amount · rent roll or lease summary · estimated taxes/insurance · target hold plan. Tools on this site can help you draft the ratio before the call.

Bridge · temporary capital path

Constraint → Bridge → Defined Exit

Bridge financing is short-term capital for a dated event: close the purchase, stabilize, then refinance or sell. The product is only as strong as the exit that repays it. Private-lender and hard-money style options may be among the paths RECR helps evaluate.

  1. ConstraintYou need capital now for acquisition, gap, or transitional hold.
  2. Bridge pathShort-term structure sized to the event, not to a 30-year hold.
  3. StabilizationRepairs, lease-up, or paperwork that unlocks the permanent solution.
  4. Exit eventSale that closes or refinance that funds — with a realistic date.
  • Best use Fast acquisition, short reposition, or carry until permanent debt funds
  • Non-negotiable A named exit strategy and a timeline you can defend
  • Sponsor Experience and liquidity matter because soft exits create expensive problems
  • Leverage & term Confirmed per private-lender program — no published marketing ranges here
  • Speed myth Complete files move faster than incomplete ones; no guaranteed closing slogans
  • RECR role Stress-test the exit, clarify the path, connect you to fitting short-term capital options

Bridge usually works when

  • The permanent loan or sale is already mapped
  • Hold costs and timeline are honest
  • The event that ends the bridge is specific

Bridge usually fails when

  • Exit is “we’ll figure it out after close”
  • Timeline assumes perfect appraisals and buyers
  • The file needs permanent cash-flow debt, not a bridge
Ground-Up · build lifecycle

Land → Plans → Budget → Draws → Exit

New construction financing is a project, not a purchase. Capital follows plans, a defensible budget, builder credibility, inspection-based draws, and a sale or permanent takeout at completion. Numeric LTC/LTV and rates stay on the program sheet — not in a brochure headline.

  1. SiteLand or lot basis and entitlement reality.
  2. PlansDrawings and specs the build will actually follow.
  3. BudgetHard costs, soft costs, contingency — line by line.
  4. ReviewBuilder track record and project economics.
  5. DrawsFunds release against verified completed work.
  6. ExitSale of finished asset or permanent / DSCR takeout.
  • Capital use Eligible land/lot, hard costs, and soft costs on a defined investor build
  • Interest reserve May be used so carry is planned during construction — deal-specific
  • Builder Licensed, credible GC is weighed heavily; policy confirmed per program
  • Timeline Schedule must fit term plus a window to sell or refinance
  • Leverage Confirmed against as-completed value and budget — not published as a fixed max
  • RECR role Organize the package and match construction-capable private-lender paths

Documents that usually unlock a real conversation

Plans or detailed scope · itemized budget · builder info · timeline · as-completed value support · exit (sale vs. refinance). Incomplete drawings and vague contingencies slow every desk.

Small Multifamily · property-type path

Duplex · Triplex · Fourplex

This path is for 2–4 unit residential investment property — not 5+ apartment or commercial CRE. Think unit rents, realistic expenses, and a hold or refinance plan. Qualification is often DSCR-style; the dedicated rental briefing covers the ratio math in more depth.

Duplex

Owner may live in one unit only if the loan remains investment/business-purpose where required — most RECR paths are non-owner-occupied. Two rent streams, simpler operations.

Triplex / Fourplex

More units, more expense realism required. Insurance and maintenance assumptions break thin deals faster than pretty exteriors fix them.

  • Launch scope Residential investment 2–4 units within RECR’s 1–4 unit universe
  • Outside scope 5+ unit and commercial apartment financing are not current launch offerings
  • Underwriting lens Unit matrix: beds, baths, rent, vacancy, expenses
  • Qualification Often cash-flow / DSCR-oriented rather than consumer DTI
  • Use of funds Purchase, rate-term refinance, or light value-add depending on program
  • RECR role Clarify property-type fit, package unit economics, match investor financing options

Practical deal snapshot

Unit rent roll · taxes/insurance estimate · deferred maintenance · entity ownership · hold vs. refinance goal. If the question is “does coverage clear?”, open the Rental / DSCR briefing next from the selector.

Purchase & Rehab · capital plan

One Path for Basis + Scope

Distinct from a pure flip story: the emphasis is how purchase price and a major renovation budget stack into one capital plan — contribution, ARV support, draw mechanics, and whether the exit is hold, sale, or refinance. No fixed “100%” or ARV-percentage marketing claims.

At closing

Acquisition portion per program rules — what portion of purchase and closing costs can be financed is confirmed per lender/program.

During rehab

Approved budget released in draws against inspections or verified milestones — not a blank check at day one.

Through exit

Sale, refinance, or DSCR takeout planned up front so the stack has somewhere to go when work finishes.

  • Vs. Fix & Flip Same renovation discipline; briefing focuses on combined capital plan and draw mechanics
  • Budget Line-item rehab — contingency is part of seriousness, not optional color
  • ARV / cost Cost-to-ARV is reviewed as a planning relationship; thresholds are program-specific
  • Contribution Borrower cash and reserves confirmed per private-lender program — never assumed zero
  • Market language Hard-money and private-lender options may fit when short-term value-add capital is the right tool
  • RECR role Align numbers, surface path options, connect the file without inventing leverage slogans

Strong purchase-rehab files

  • Basis and scope are both written down
  • ARV comps match the finished product
  • Draw schedule matches how work actually happens

Weak purchase-rehab files

  • Purchase price “works” only if rehab is free
  • Budget is a single round number
  • Exit is optional or undefined
