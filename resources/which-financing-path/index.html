Which Investor Financing Path Fits This Deal?

Start with the exit and the work — not the product brochure. This guide is a decision framework across RECR’s six launch paths for business-purpose, non-owner-occupied residential investment property (principally 1–4 units). RECR helps evaluate path fit and connect private-lender options; it is not automatically the direct funder.

Four questions that choose the path

Most wrong-product files fail one of these questions before any underwriter opens a checklist:

  1. What event retires the capital? Sale, refinance into a hold, or permanent takeout after construction? If you cannot name the exit, short-term money is a countdown without a door.
  2. What work happens before that exit? Cosmetic flip, major renovation, new construction, or already-stabilized rental? Work type drives draw structure and documentation.
  3. What is the property type? SFR/condo investment, duplex–fourplex, or a true build? Property type changes income tests and eligibility, not just the marketing label.
  4. What is the timing problem? Permanent money is ready later; acquisition is now; lease-up is incomplete — that is bridge logic. Permanent cash-flow money for a performing rental is DSCR logic.

Answer those in plain English before you shop for “hard money” or “DSCR.” Those market words describe private-lender segments; they are not substitutes for an exit plan.

Map of the six launch paths

Fix & Flip

Buy → renovate → sell (or refinance only if the hold plan is real). Capital use is purchase plus rehab with a resale exit. Deepen ARV with understanding ARV and draws with rehab draws.

Purchase & Rehab

Acquisition and major renovation stack into one capital plan — basis, budget, contribution, exit. Not the same story as a pure flip sale narrative. Compare carefully in fix-and-flip vs purchase-and-rehab.

Bridge Financing

Short-term capital for a dated event: close now, stabilize, then refinance or sell. The product is the exit. Read bridge exit risk and, when the takeout is a hold loan, bridge-to-DSCR.

Rental / DSCR

Longer-term financing for investment rentals evaluated on property income vs debt service, not consumer DTI theater. Use the DSCR calculator and DSCR explained.

Small Multifamily (2–4)

Property-type owner for duplex, triplex, fourplex residential investment. Qualification is often DSCR-style; the multifamily page owns the asset type, rental owns the coverage structure. Not 5+ apartments.

Ground-Up Construction

Plans, budget, builder credibility, draws, completion, then sale or permanent takeout. See the construction budget guide. Numeric LTC/rates stay on program sheets — not on this site as marketing floors.

Short-term vs longer-term capital

Short-term paths (flip, bridge, many purchase-rehab and construction stacks) are underwritten to a finish line. Delay is not cosmetic; it is maturity risk. Longer-term rental paths are underwritten to whether the property can carry the payment after taxes, insurance, and realistic vacancy — which is why a “refi later” plan must survive a DSCR-style test before you treat it as an exit.

Illustrative (not a RECR term sheet): if you buy a rental that needs six months of work before it leases, short-term capital may carry the work period; DSCR-style money may fit only after income is real. That sequence is the heart of bridge-to-DSCR thinking.

Common mismatches

  • Calling every rehab a flip when the real plan is hold — income math never gets done until the short-term money is already outstanding.
  • Calling every timing problem a bridge without naming the takeout — see exit risk.
  • Forcing a fourplex into “commercial” — RECR’s launch multifamily scope is 2–4 residential units; commercial CRE is deferred.
  • Treating ground-up as a big flip without plans, contingency, and a completion exit.
  • Shopping hard-money labels before total project cost and exit are written down — start with preparing a deal submission.

What to do next

1) Write the four answers above. 2) Open the matching program page. 3) Run the relevant tool (MAO, DSCR, rehab budget, checklist). 4) If the path is still ambiguous, submit the file for a path conversation — not a rate fantasy.
